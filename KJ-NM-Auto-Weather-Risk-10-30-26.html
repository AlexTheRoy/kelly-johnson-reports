03

Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution

What goes into the score
  • HailNRI hail risk: how often damaging hail falls times the value exposed, scored 0 to 100 against every U.S. census tract. Hail is the classic comprehensive claim, so it carries half the weight.
  • WindNRI strong wind risk: damaging straight-line wind, which brings falling limbs, blowing debris and broken glass.
  • WildfireNRI wildfire risk: wildland fire likelihood times exposure. Dense urban tracts with no wildland fuel carry no FEMA wildfire rating and are scored 0 here.
  • FloodNRI inland flood risk: river and flash flooding, including monsoon runoff through arroyos. A flooded vehicle is a comprehensive claim.
  • AvailabilityInsurer rating models are vehicle- and garage-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same hazards. Tornado, winter weather and lightning are in the spreadsheet but not scored: tornado risk is low in most of the state, and winter weather drives collision rather than comprehensive claims.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeComposite score 75+
  • HighComposite score 65+, or a ZIP hail score of 80+ on its own
  • ModerateComposite score 50+
  • LowEverything else. The cut points are set within New Mexico, so Low still means real hail exposure: the lowest-scoring Albuquerque ZIPs still rank above the national middle for hail.
  • How Extreme is setExtreme marks about one household in 6 (16.1% of households): ZIPs where several hazards run high at once, most often hail with wildfire or flood.

Auto quoting by tier
  • ExtremeComprehensive review. Recommend comprehensive on every vehicle, including older ones still worth repairing; ask where each vehicle is parked (garage, carport or street); walk through the comprehensive deductible and glass coverage.
  • HighComprehensive check. On any vehicle quoted liability-only, offer comprehensive and show the price difference; mention the spring and summer storm season.
  • ModerateStandard quote. Comprehensive is still worth offering on any financed or newer vehicle.
  • LowStandard quote.
These tiers describe where a coverage conversation matters most, not where to stop advertising. Drivers in High and Extreme ZIPs are the ones who most need comprehensive, and offering it is good service. We do not recommend excluding any ZIP on weather exposure. Comprehensive in the conversation

Questions that make a New Mexico Auto quote faster and stickier

Why it matters here
  • HailA single hailstorm can total an older vehicle or crack every pane of glass. Without comprehensive, the driver pays for it. Where hail scores run high, asking “where do you park?” and quoting comprehensive by default shows the client you know the area.
  • Wind and dustStrong wind brings falling limbs, blowing debris and pitted glass. The Southeast and Eastern Plains average wind scores of 77 and 83.
  • Wildfire and floodFire and flood damage to a vehicle are comprehensive claims too. In mountain and river-valley ZIPs, these are the reasons to keep comprehensive on a paid-off vehicle.
  • TornadoTornado risk is low across most of the state; it is highest on the Eastern Plains, averaging 62.
  • Winter weatherWinter weather scores average 84 statewide. Ice and snow drive collision claims rather than comprehensive, which is a natural moment to review collision and deductibles as well.

Data & files

  • Risk sourceFEMA National Risk Index, census tract table, December 2025 release (hail, strong wind, wildfire, inland flooding, tornado, winter weather, lightning), all 612 New Mexico tracts.
  • GeographyU.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and the 2020 ZCTA-to-tract relationship file. The file reports land-area overlaps, not population, so each tract's values are weighted into a ZIP by the tract's population times the share of its land inside the ZIP. Tract parts outside New Mexico are not scored. Household counts from ACS 2020-2024.
  • What NRI measuresNRI risk scores combine how often a hazard occurs, the value exposed and community resilience, ranked 0 to 100 against all U.S. census tracts. They describe buildings, people and agriculture, not vehicles specifically, so they are a guide to where weather hits hardest rather than a vehicle claim rate.
  • LeadsAll 521 GOAL Auto lead records for this account, leads dated June 22 to October 6, 2026, counted by the ZIP on each lead; sold means the lead is marked sold in GOAL from your status uploads (26). 517 leads matched a populated New Mexico ZIP; 4 used PO box or business ZIPs with no Census profile. Only counts by ZIP are used. Call topics are counts of inbound call summaries that mention each topic.
  • LimitsA ZIP score describes the ZIP, not a vehicle. Where it is parked, its value and how it is driven decide each quote; the tiers decide where to ask first.
  • FilesKJ-NM-ZIP-Data-10-30-26.csv · every ZIP, every score, tier and treatment column, plus the demographics used in the companion report and your Auto lead and sold counts by ZIP.
